VOLTAGE DROP AND BATTERY BACKUP CALCULATIONS — BAZspark Engineering Reference

SECTION 1: VOLTAGE DROP CALCULATIONS

1.1 General Principles
Per NFPA 72 and standard electrical engineering practice, the voltage at the most remote device on any circuit must remain within the device's rated operating range under worst-case conditions.

1.2 Standard Formulas

TOTAL WIRE RESISTANCE:
  R_total = 2 × L × R_unit
  Where:
    L      = one-way cable length (meters)
    R_unit = resistance per meter for selected wire gauge (Ω/m)

WIRE RESISTANCE TABLE (copper, at 20°C):
  AWG 18: 0.02067 Ω/m (most common for SLC/Class B IDC)
  AWG 16: 0.01300 Ω/m
  AWG 14: 0.00820 Ω/m (common NAC/IDC run)
  AWG 12: 0.00515 Ω/m (heavy NAC runs, long distances)
  AWG 10: 0.00324 Ω/m (main supply feeders)

VOLTAGE DROP:
  V_drop = I_total × R_total
  Where I_total = sum of all device currents on the circuit (amperes)

TERMINAL VOLTAGE:
  V_terminal = V_supply - V_drop
  This must be ≥ V_device_min (e.g., 20.4 V for 24 VDC devices, 10.2 V for 12 VDC)

1.3 Maximum Allowable Drop
Per common industry practice and UL listing:
  - For 24 VDC systems: V_terminal ≥ 20.4 V (15% drop from nominal)
  - For 12 VDC systems: V_terminal ≥ 10.2 V (15% drop from nominal)

1.4 Example Calculation
  System: 24 VDC NAC
  Cable: 14 AWG (R_unit = 0.00820 Ω/m)
  One-way length: 150 m
  Total device current: 0.80 A

  R_total = 2 × 150 × 0.00820 = 2.46 Ω
  V_drop  = 0.80 × 2.46 = 1.97 V
  V_terminal = 24.0 - 1.97 = 22.03 V ✓ (≥ 20.4 V — PASS)

SECTION 2: BATTERY BACKUP CALCULATIONS

2.1 NFPA 72 Requirements
Per NFPA 72 Section 10.6.7.2:
  - Standby power: Minimum 24 hours of standby (supervisory) mode
  - Alarm power: Minimum 5 minutes of full alarm at end of standby period
  - For high-rise or special hazard: 24 h standby + 15 min alarm minimum

2.2 Battery Capacity Formula
  Battery AH required = (I_standby × T_standby) + (I_alarm × T_alarm) × Safety_factor

  Safety_factor = 1.20 (20% derating for aging and temperature effects)

  Where:
    I_standby = panel + detector quiescent current (amperes)
    T_standby = standby time (hours, typically 24)
    I_alarm   = full alarm current (all appliances active) (amperes)
    T_alarm   = alarm time (hours, typically 0.0833 h = 5 min)

2.3 Example Calculation
  I_standby = 0.35 A (all detectors + panel supervisory)
  I_alarm   = 4.20 A (all NAC devices active + panel)
  T_standby = 24 h
  T_alarm   = 0.0833 h (5 min)

  Battery AH = (0.35 × 24 + 4.20 × 0.0833) × 1.20
             = (8.40 + 0.350) × 1.20
             = 8.75 × 1.20
             = 10.50 AH → Select next standard size: 12 AH battery

2.4 Temperature Derating
  Battery capacity degrades at low temperatures:
  - 20°C: 100% capacity
  - 0°C:  87% capacity
  - -10°C: 76% capacity
  Apply further derating if installation is in unheated space.

SECTION 3: SIGNALING LINE CIRCUIT (SLC) LOOP RESISTANCE

3.1 General
  SLC (Class A or B) maximum loop resistance is defined by FACP manufacturer.
  Typical limits: 40 Ω to 100 Ω total loop resistance.

3.2 Class B (Style 4) Wiring
  R_loop = R_unit × total_length (one way, since Class B is open circuit at break)
  Example: AWG 18, 600 m one-way = 600 × 0.02067 = 12.40 Ω (within 40 Ω limit)

3.3 Class A (Style 6/7) Wiring
  Both conductors are looped back; resistance on each conductor must meet limits.
  Consult FACP data sheet for maximum values.
